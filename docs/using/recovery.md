# Getting back in

> [!NOTE]
> Needs Viauna 0.7 or newer, and a server whose administrator has turned it on. If you
> don't see **Recovery** in Settings > Accounts, ask them.

If you forget your password, Viauna can get you back in, as long as you set up a way
beforehand. There are two, and you can have both:

- **A recovery address**: another email address of yours, at another provider. A code
  is sent there when you need one.
- **Backup codes**: ten codes to print or save, each good for one reset.

## Setting it up

Open **Settings > Accounts**, and under **Recovery**:

- **Add a recovery address**: enter it and your password. A code is sent to it; enter the
  code under Recovery to confirm it's yours. Until then it isn't used.
- **Make backup codes**: enter your password, and save the ten codes that appear: copy
  them, download them or print them. They're shown only once. Keep them somewhere safe,
  away from your computer, such as a printed sheet in a drawer.

Your mailbox gets a notice each time either changes, so if someone else ever adds one,
you'll know. Remove the address, or make new codes (which stops the old ones working),
from the same place.

Whoever can read mail at your recovery address, or holds your backup codes, can choose a
new password for your mailbox. Pick an address you keep secure, and treat the codes like
a spare key.

## Forgot your password?

1. On the sign-in page, enter your address and choose **Forgot password?**
2. Choose **Next**. If you have a recovery address, a code is sent to it; it works for 15
   minutes.
3. Enter that code, or one of your backup codes (in capitals or not, with or without the
   dash). Each backup code works once.
4. Choose a new password, at least 8 characters. The server may refuse one that has
   appeared in a data breach.

Then sign in with the new password. Every browser that was signed in to your mailbox is
signed out, your other mail apps (on your phone, Outlook and so on) will ask for the new
password, and a notice goes to your mailbox and your recovery address.

The page answers the same whether or not an address has recovery set up, so it can't be
used to find out who has.

## A reset link

With neither set up, ask whoever runs your mail. They can make you a **reset link**,
which works once, for 24 hours. Open it, and choose your new password: they never see
it. Treat the link like a password until you've used it.
